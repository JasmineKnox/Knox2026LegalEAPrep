# Legal EA: Casefile — Version 1 Source Map

This file records the source basis used when building the student-facing game content.

## Current curriculum authority

**QCAA Legal Studies 2025 v1.3 General senior syllabus — January 2026**

Key points used in Version 1:

- External assessment assesses Objectives **1, 3 and 4**.
- External assessment relates to **Unit 4 Topic 1: Human rights** and **Unit 4 Topic 3: Human rights in Australian contexts**.
- Objective 3 analysis: determine **nature and scope**, examine **viewpoints and their consequences**.
- Objective 4 evaluation: present **legal alternatives**, make a **recommendation**, justify through **legal criteria**, discuss **implications**.
- Topic 1 includes human-rights terminology, the International Bill of Human Rights, principal treaties, treaty ratification/accession, the Human Rights (Parliamentary Scrutiny) Act 2011 (Cth), state rights under the UN Charter and the constitutional external affairs power.
- Topic 3 includes Australian human-rights contexts, the right to protest/freedom of assembly, discrimination, democratic representation, religion, jury trial, complaint pathways and the roles of human-rights organisations, courts, law-reform bodies and media.

## Treaty Process

Based primarily on:

- `Week 3 EA Prep Legal 2026.pptx`
- `Walk_the_Treaty_Teacher_Resource_Pack.docx`
- 2025 QCAA EA short-response marking guide for treaty ratification

Accuracy locks retained:

1. Signature is not ratification.
2. Ratification creates the international legal commitment.
3. Treaty obligations do not automatically become enforceable domestic Australian law; legislation may be required.

The classroom pathway is a teaching sequence. Real treaty-making can involve more detailed procedural variation.

## EA Boss Battle — Port Calder: Protest Zone

**All Port Calder Bill text and stakeholder statements are fictional classroom stimuli.**

The scenario is designed to rehearse the current EA reasoning chain:

**nature/scope → viewpoint 1 + consequence → viewpoint 2 + consequence → stimulus evidence → two legal alternatives → recommendation + legal criteria → implication**

The simulation assumption about peaceful assembly is based on the human-rights/protest framework used in the supplied 2025 QCAA EA stimulus and teacher EA-preparation materials. It is presented explicitly as a simulation assumption rather than as a claim that the fictional Bill or facts are current law.

## Legal criteria

The V1 game uses the current syllabus categories:

- just and/or equitable outcomes
- relevant elements of the rule of law
- balancing protection of individual rights with protection of society

Other teacher criteria may still be useful in classroom discussion where they genuinely connect to one of the syllabus categories and the legal issue.
